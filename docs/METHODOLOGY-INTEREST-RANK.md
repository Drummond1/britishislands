# How a filter orders its list

The atlas list used to put a photograph first and then sort A to Z, or, for whisky, by region. That buried the thing the filter is for.

`interest-rank.js` scores each island inside the active filter. A higher score is higher on the list. Equal scores fall back to the island name. The score is not shown.

A search still wins. Typing a name ranks by the match, not by this order.

The same score picks the cited row on the card and the first rows inside the island profile. Ask uses the same order when it answers from a collection.

The static pages under `/collections/` still list islands by how many citations they carry. They are a reference list, not the browse order.

Nothing here is a popularity chart or a count of visitors. The signals are the cited row itself, whether the picture is of that subject, and a few facts already on the island (area, a photograph, sea or river).

## Unusual news

The event is the point, not the size of the island. A report about a declaration, a vanishing, an evacuation, anthrax, a whisky wreck, Castaway, forty days on a rock, a feudal election, the beach runway, or a cable car outranks a local oddity. A second story on the same island is a small extra. Gruinard, Lundy, and Eigg lead because each has two of those reports.

## Islands with castles

A photographed castle on a small island outranks a castle that is one building on a large island. A mansion or a country house is ranked down. A named castle people cross the water to see (Eilean Donan, Kisimul, Castle Tioram, Lindisfarne) is ranked up. Oxford Castle is ranked down because that pin is the city, not an island.

## Rare wildlife

A species that carries the island's name, or is endemic, or is a subspecies the page ties to that island, outranks a widespread animal whose article merely mentions the island among other countries. An extinct animal is ranked below a living one on the same island. A picture of the species counts. The St Kilda wren leads that island, and St Kilda leads the list because several such species are cited there.

## Whisky

A working still outranks a silent still, a story, or a plan. A picture of the still counts. A well-known name (Bowmore, Talisker, Highland Park, Jura, and the other stills in that set) counts. Extra working stills on the same island count a little, so Islay leads and a single obscure still does not. Whisky Galore and the Politician are stories, so they sit below the working stills.

## Books

A picture of the book outranks a stand-in photograph of the island. A novel or memoir counts. A book a reader is likely to know (Kidnapped, Catriona, The Blackhouse, Toilers of the Sea, The Outrun, and the others in that set) counts more than a longer pile of titles.

## Local traditions

A custom people would recognise outranks a long list of lesser ones. Up Helly Aa, Tynwald, the Ba, the Papa Stour sword dance, the guga hunt, and the St Kilda parliament and mailboat sit above a craft or a food, even when the only open picture is of the island. A picture of the custom still helps.

## Tidal islands

The cited sentence is the same shape for every island, so the text cannot separate them. A known crossing (Lindisfarne, St Michael's Mount, Burgh, Cramond, Erraid, Omey, and the others in that set) leads. A photograph of the island helps. An island that also has a castle, a book, a custom, or a news story helps, because there is something there besides the tide. A river ait is ranked down.

## Film, art and music

A film outranks a piece of music, which outranks one artwork. A picture of the work counts. A known work (Highlander, Macbeth, Father Ted, Fingal's Cave, Whisky Galore, and the others in that set) counts. A row whose text is only a Commons category or a MusicBrainz stub is ranked down, so a pile of untitled pictures does not lead.

## For sale

The list does not carry a whole-island flag on the card, so the order uses the listing title and the island. A sea island with a photograph, small enough to be the thing for sale, leads. A title that says private island, whole island, or an acre estate leads further. A house, a bungalow, or a home on a river ait is ranked down. Prices are not used.

## Remote

Remote is the uninhabited sea islands with no ferry. An island that already has a cited story, and a photograph, leads. St Kilda, Rockall, North Rona, Staffa, and Sùla Sgeir get a further lift because they are the outposts the word remote refers to, and Rockall has no area stored to lift it any other way. Great Britain and Ireland are ranked down.

## All islands

The unfiltered list, and any browse that is not a search or one of the filters above, follows the strongest cited story. A news report, a castle, a film, a book, a still, a custom, a species, and a tidal crossing are put on one scale, using the same judgement as that filter. A second story adds a little. It does not add a point per category.

Area and population are not the order. A large island leads only when its story is the stronger one. A photograph of the subject adds a small amount, so a story you can see sits just above the same story with no picture. More photographs do not move an island further up. Great Britain and Ireland stay at the bottom.

The first screen runs Erraid, Lindisfarne, Lewis and Harris, Islay, Guernsey, Papa Westray, Eilean Donan, Mull, Bass Rock, Burgh Island, Davaar, and Omey. The three cards above the list are the first three of that same order.
